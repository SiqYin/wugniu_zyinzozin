The Wu common language is a mixed dialect based on two closely related Wu dialects — mid-generation Suzhounese and old Shanghainese — and incorporates phonological features of various northern Wu dialects. Its phonology and character readings have undergone editorial review and phonetic standardization. The phonological system of the “Wu common language” is grounded in old Shanghainese and mid-generation Suzhounese. The tonal system and tone sandhi are based on mid-generation Suzhounese while remaining compatible with mid-generation Shanghainese variants. Individual character readings are based on Shanghainese and Suzhounese, cross-checked against rhyme books, with colloquial readings given priority. At the same time, innovations shared exclusively by Suzhounese and Shanghainese (i.e., innovations not found in other Wu dialects) are removed and replaced with mainstream northern Wu readings⁴. The vocabulary embraces both northern and southern Wu, and permits a wide range of dialectal vocabulary. The grammar, while grounded in northern Wu, draws heavily on southern Wu to restore traditional Wu grammar and reduce Mandarin influence.
